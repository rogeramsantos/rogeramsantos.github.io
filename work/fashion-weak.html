fashion weAk

Helping people with a minimal wardrobe wear colour with confidence

ux research / product design / mobile app

fashion weAk is a wardrobe app for people who are a bit “weak at fashion”. In a two-week solo project, I researched why people with small wardrobes keep reaching for black and navy, then designed an app that finds your colour palette and builds outfits from clothes you already own.

{ challenge }

People with a small wardrobe stick to dark “safe” colours. Not because they dislike colour, but because they can’t tell which colours suit them, and they only find out after buying. The challenge: help them choose colour with confidence, without adding more choice, shopping or clutter.

{ impact }

3people interviewed

{ approach }

A solo project, end to end: interviews, affinity mapping, persona, journey map, MoSCoW, information architecture, low-fi to hi-fi, and concept testing with three users. I built the prototype on a real test wardrobe, so every outfit uses clothes that actually exist.

{ users }

01

Meet Jamie, “The Safe Dresser”

One persona, built from the interviews: someone who doesn’t care much about clothes, but still doesn’t want to look bad.

Persona

Jamie, 30, Berlin

Freelancer with a small, practical wardrobe. Gets dressed in 2–3 minutes by sticking to dark colours he knows “work”, and asks his sister or partner for a second opinion. Uses Pinterest, YouTube and Google; shops at Uniqlo and secondhand.

Goals & pain points

What he needs

Know which colours suit him, wear more colour without standing out, and get more outfits from what he owns. The pain: he only finds out a colour doesn’t work after buying it, or right as he leaves the house.

02

Listening to safe dressers

I interviewed three people with minimal wardrobes (P1–P3) and clustered their quotes into an affinity diagram of 16 themes, from colour and comfort to other people’s opinions.

What they said
  • “With black on black you can’t go wrong.” — P2
  • “Even though the fit was okay, the color was off, so I passed.” — P3
  • “I try to basically minimize the choice a lot.” — P1
  • “I don’t want to attract much attention… but I also don’t want to look bad.” — P1
  • “It’s just frustrating when there’s a clash of opinions.” — P3

{ testing }

05

What testers pushed back on

I ran concept tests with three users. Five things changed before the final design.

Before feedback
  • The home screen looked like a profile page
  • Exact hex codes felt technical
  • Items were sorted by upload order
  • A temperature filter felt too rigid
  • “Where else do I wear this item?”
After feedback
  • Home became a daily dashboard: weather + today’s clothes
  • Broad colour names: Sage green, Mustard, Indigo
  • Wardrobe filters by item type
  • Outfits filter by season
  • Item detail shows the outfits that use it

{ solution }

06

Key features

The final design focuses on three features, all built around one idea: wear more colour using what you already own.

  1. Find your colours →Scan your face to get a personal palette: Ivory, Olive, Petrol blue, Terracotta, Ochre…
  2. Outfits from a proven systemCombinations from Sanzo Wada’s Dictionary of Color Combinations, using only items you own.
  3. Today’s clothesThe home screen suggests an outfit for the Berlin weather, straight from your wardrobe.
